About this data

I started collecting these listings to make the library, archives, and museum job search a little less scattered, and this page grew out of that: a running record of what a handful of job boards have posted since November 2025. It's useful for getting a feel for the field, but please read it as a picture of these boards, not of the whole job market.

A few things to keep in mind:

  • It only sees what these boards publish. Plenty of employers post only on their own websites, on general job sites, or on boards I can't collect from automatically, including ALA JobLIST and AAM JobHQ. Academic jobs are overrepresented; public library, school, corporate, and many government jobs are underrepresented.
  • There are gaps. The collector ran on a Raspberry Pi until June 23, 2026, and was offline from then until October 31, 2026, when I moved it to GitHub. Nothing was collected in between. The first month and the month collection resumed include every job that was already open, so they run high; the month it went offline runs low.
  • The sources have changed. On October 31, 2026, I added the Simmons SLIS Jobline, the CLIR+DLF Job Board, Code4Lib, the Digital Preservation Coalition, USAJOBS, and keyword searches on the Chronicle and Inside Higher Ed. Counts from that date on aren't directly comparable with earlier ones.
  • Salaries are only what's listed. Most postings don't include pay. Boards that require it, like CLIR+DLF and USAJOBS, are overrepresented in the salary figures, so treat the medians as rough, and the medians by job family as rougher still.
  • Some details are worked out automatically. Job families, job types, remote work, locations, and duplicate postings are all inferred from titles and posting text. It's right most of the time, not all of the time, and I'd rather you know that than trust a category too far.
  • A posting isn't a hire. A job that's reposted counts again, and a listing that disappears may have been filled, canceled, or just taken down. "How long postings stay up" only uses the boards whose feeds show every open job, and it reflects how long employers pay to keep a listing up as much as anything else. It isn't a measure of how long it takes to hire.

If you use these numbers, please describe them as approximate and say where they came from. If something looks wrong, or you know of a board I should add, I'd love to hear about it: open an issue on GitHub.
